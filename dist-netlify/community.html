HR

Post it here

Anything with a human on the other end: how do I get this fixture patched, why is my Art-Net not arriving, here is a show file, here is a look I built, here is a plugin I wrote, is this a bug or is it me. Screenshots welcome — they usually answer the question faster than the words do.

GH

Open a GitHub issue instead

Once something is reproducible and clearly wrong — a crash, a fixture profile with the wrong channel order, a feature that does not do what the guide says — it belongs in the tracker where it can be closed by a commit. Post here first if you are not sure; someone will tell you which it is.

The five chips

Where your thread goes.

Pick a category when you post and the chips above the thread list will filter to it. None of them are policed — pick the closest one and carry on.

What belongs in each.

  • General — rigs you are proud of, gear you are eyeing, what you ran last weekend, everything that is not one of the four below.
  • Help — "this is not doing what I expect." Say what you did, what happened, and what you wanted instead, and attach the screenshot.
  • Show files — a config or a stage setup you want other people to open. Say what rig it assumes and how many universes it fills.
  • Plugins — scripts you wrote or found. Post the link here for feedback; submit it to the registry when it is ready for everyone.
  • Bugs — something is broken and you can make it happen again. Include your platform and roughly what you were doing.

House rules

How the board works.

Four things to know before your first post. There is no application, no moderation queue and no email list.

RD

Reading needs no account

Every thread is public. Browse, search by category, open anything, link to it. Signing in only matters when you want to write something — and a thread's link works for anyone you send it to.

ID

One username, chosen by you

Sign in with Google, then pick a handle: 3 to 24 letters, numbers, dashes or underscores. That handle is what appears on your posts. Your Google account name never does.

IMG

One screenshot per post

PNG, JPEG or WebP, up to 4 MB, on a thread or on any reply. Click the image in a post to open it full size. Crop to the part that matters — the Inspector, the channel row, the Network checklist.

DEL

You can delete your own

A delete link sits on anything you wrote. Removing a thread takes its replies with it. Nobody else can touch your posts, and nothing you delete stays behind in someone else's view.

Posts are plain text and line breaks are kept, so paste a log excerpt or a snippet of JSON straight in. Titles run to 120 characters and a post to 10,000. Threads sort by the most recent reply, so a good answer pulls an old question back to the top.

Show files

Share a rig or a whole show.

DMXexpress writes everything as human-readable JSON, so sharing a show is a matter of finding the right file and pasting it into a post.

  1. 1

    Save it first

    A complete show is saved as a config; a reusable arrangement of fixtures, truss and towers is saved as a setup. Give it a name you would recognise in a list of other people's rigs.

  2. 2

    Find the file

    Configs live under configs/ and setups under setups/, inside your show-data folder. Both are plain JSON you can open in any editor before you hand them over.

  3. 3

    Post it where it can be downloaded

    The board takes text and one image per post, not arbitrary files — put the JSON in a GitHub gist or repository and paste the link into a Show files thread.

  4. 4

    Say what it assumes

    Which fixtures it patches, how many universes it fills, whether the stage layout matters. A setup built around eight movers on a truss is useless to someone with four pars unless you say so.

Where your show data lives
# Windows
%APPDATA%\DMXpress\configs\
%APPDATA%\DMXpress\setups\

# macOS
~/Library/Application Support/DMXpress/configs/

# Linux
~/.local/share/DMXpress/configs/

Leave inspector.json out of it.

Panel preferences — which Inspector tab is open, how wide it sits, which sections are folded, what the stage draws — belong to your machine, not to your show. They are kept out of saved configurations on purpose, so a show you download never rearranges your workspace.
