How to use it

New · The right-hand panel

The Inspector: five tabs that run the rig.

Stored looks, the selection you are holding, the camera, the truss, the patch — all of it in one column you never have to go hunting for. Press Alt + 1…5 to jump between tabs, Alt + I to fold the panel away, or roll the wheel over the tab strip.

Under the strip a context line always says what is picked — “6 lights · SlimPAR ×6”, “F34 truss 2 · 4 / 24 slots” — with the live colour beside it. Click it to jump to Selection, press the ✕ to drop the selection, or right-click it for select-all and same-type.

  • Presets — stored looks as coloured pads, filed in folders, with a board that matches the deck
  • Selection — move, align, aim and hang the lights you have picked
  • Stage — cameras, quick views, and what the stage draws
  • Build — towers, truss and whole structures in one press
  • Patch — library, addressing, naming and placement in one pass

The panel's own state — the open tab, the width, which sections are folded, what the stage draws — lives in inspector.json and is deliberately left out of saved show configurations. Loading another show never rearranges your workspace.

The tab strip and the context line — the line under the tabs always says what is picked.

No rig? No problem.

Practice at home with zero physical lights.

The built-in 3D visualizer renders your entire rig — fixture positions, colors, movement, and volumetric beams — live as you program. That means you can learn the console, build full shows, and rehearse busking sessions from your desk.

When you're ready for the real thing, the exact same show outputs over Art-Net to physical nodes and fixtures. What you rehearsed is what you run.

  • Watch every change instantly in the 3D stage
  • Move, aim, and color fixtures with on-stage gizmos
  • Volumetric beams show real look and haze feel
  • Flip the same show to live Art-Net output when it counts

Inspector · Presets

Your looks, as pads you can read at a glance.

Build a look in the Programmer, type a name, press Store look. It lands as a pad that takes its colour from the values inside it — a warm wash is amber, a cold blue is blue — so you find the one you want by looking, not by reading.

Drop pads into folders, pin the handful you reach for all night, and search the rest. Recall is yours to set: cut straight to it, fade over a number of seconds you choose, or follow whatever the Transition window is doing. Blackout sits at the bottom of the tab.

  • Pad colour comes from the stored values, not from a swatch you had to pick
  • Badges mark the looks carrying an oscillator, a phaser or a colour cycle
  • Folders, pins and a search box for a night's worth of looks
  • Recall cuts, fades over n seconds, or follows the transition setting

Inspector · Presets board

A board that matches the deck under your hands.

The board button opens a page of pads laid out exactly like the Stream Deck's Presets page. What you arrange on screen is what your thumbs will find on the keys — and if there is no deck plugged in, you still get the board.

Turn Arrange on and the page edits itself in place. Click a pad to point it at a preset. Drag one pad onto another to swap them. Right-click to clear one. Every pad gets its own colour, a four-letter label and a picture.

  • Click a pad to choose which preset it fires
  • Drag pads to swap them, right-click to clear a slot
  • Per-pad colour, four-letter label and artwork
  • Fill lays a whole folder out across the page in one go
  • Pads hold preset IDs, so renaming or reordering the pool never re-points one at the wrong look

The Presets board in Arrange mode, one slot's colour, label and artwork open for editing.

Hardware busking

Plug in a Stream Deck.
The show is on the keys.

DMXexpress drives every Elgato Stream Deck natively over USB — no Elgato software, no plugins. The keys mirror what you build in the app, drawn live with names, colors, and artwork: whole-look presets, color palettes, gobos and wheel effects, your phaser pool, and all twelve chase shapes.

On the Stream Deck Plus XL the touch strip becomes a console readout — grand master level, tempo pulsing on the beat, the active page — and the knobs are real encoders.

  • Four pages on the Mode knob: Presets, Palettes, Phasers, Chases
  • The Palettes page flips between Colors, Gobos, and every effect wheel in the rig
  • Build custom phaser pages — 36 slots each, with per-key color, label, and artwork
  • Dimmer and Beat knobs ride the grand master and tap the tempo
  • A programmer knob nudges any channel on the selection; press it to step to the next
  • Hot-plug friendly: unplug mid-show and it reconnects by itself

Designing the deck's Phaser pages in the app — one tile per key, each with its own phaser, color, label, and artwork.

Inspector · Selection

Move a dozen lights like they were one.

The top card sums up what you have picked. Under it a transform grid edits X, Y and Z, yaw, pitch and size for the whole selection at once — and marks a row mixed when the selected lights disagree, so you never flatten a value you meant to keep.

Below that is the tool strip. Nudge with the pad, or with the arrow keys over the stage. Align and distribute on any axis. Mirror. Arrange the selection into a line, an arc, a circle or a grid. Aim it straight down, level, at stage centre, at a point you pick, or fanned. Set one common height for the lot. Then hang them onto a tower or a truss, choosing which face they go on and how they fill the free slots.

  • A transform grid that admits when the selection disagrees
  • Nudge, align, distribute and mirror on any axis
  • Arrange into a line, arc, circle or grid
  • Aim down, level, at stage centre, at a point, or fanned
  • Hang a whole selection onto a tower or truss and pick the face

Pick a tower or a truss on its own and the tab becomes that structure's editor instead.

Several lights picked: the summary card, a transform row reading “mixed”, and the tools below.

Inspector · Stage

Look at the rig from anywhere, in one key press.

Switch between Orbit and Fly, then jump to an angle with the quick-view pads. Frame the selection to fill the screen with the six lights you are working on, or frame the whole rig to see where you have got to.

Save an angle you like as a named camera, give it a number key, and press it over the stage to fly back. The Show block turns individual things on and off, with All, Clean and Plan presets that change the whole look in a click — Plan puts a name on every light and takes the beams away.

  • Twelve switches: floor grid, stage box, beams, pools, labels, towers, truss, slot rings, gizmo, aim ticks, effect overlays, corner help
  • Name tags cycle off, then selected lights, then every light
  • Cameras 1–9 on the number keys, straight over the stage
  • The stage box's own width, depth and height sit right underneath
  • Grid pitch, body size and beam haze dial in how heavy the picture looks

The Beat window: tap along, watch the bar, then bend time with the transport below.

Sound-reactive

The music runs the show.

Open the Audio window and pick any source your computer can hear — on Windows that includes plain system audio, so you can play a track and light it with zero cabling. DMXexpress watches the spectrum, and a beat tracker taps the tempo for you like a very patient drummer.

Then draw trigger bands straight onto the spectrum: when the bass crosses the line, throw a palette at a group. When the highs sparkle, fire a scene. Attack, release, and merge behaviour are all yours to shape.

  • System-audio loopback on Windows; microphones and virtual devices everywhere
  • Predict-and-correct beat tracking feeds the existing tap-tempo machinery
  • Band triggers: gate, one-shot, or continuous follow per band
  • Manual tap always works — the space bar taps whenever the Beat window is open
  • The time machine bends it all: reverse, half-rate, pendulum, stutter

Inspector · Build

Put the rig up in eight presses.

Eight pads build the structures a real stage is made of: a tower, a straight F34 run, a radius run, or a whole goalpost, box, ring, tower pair or arch in one press. The number keys 1…8 do the same. Standard lengths, radii, arcs and heights are one tap each, so you are picking a truss rather than typing millimetres.

Under the pads, an outliner lists every element in the rig, folding a composite's parts under one heading with the lights hung on them. Click a row to select it, then rename, hide, mirror or duplicate it, or point the camera at it. Setups at the bottom save and reload a whole named rig.

  • Eight structures, eight number keys, no mouse needed
  • An outliner that nests a composite's parts and the lights on them
  • Rename, hide, mirror, duplicate, or fly the camera to any element
  • Named setups save and reload the whole rig

Inspector · Patch

From the library to hung on the truss, without leaving the panel.

Start from your recents, a built-in profile, or the library — 2,200 models and 7,400 patchable modes from more than 200 makers, searchable by name and filtered down to moving heads, pars, bars or strobes. A preview card shows the mode, the channel count, the pan and tilt ranges, and what every channel does before you commit to it.

Set a count and a start address. Next free and first gap find one for you, skipping addresses already taken and refusing to let a fixture straddle the two universes. Name them once and they are numbered for you, continuing the series already in the rig.

  • Land a batch in a line, a grid, an arc, a circle, or on the floor
  • Or hang it straight onto a truss — which it will build to fit if you ask
  • The rig underneath is grouped by type, with how full each universe is
  • Add one more of something, select them all, or unpatch them in two clicks

It will not let a fixture straddle the join

Slots 1–512 go to the universe you set, 513–1024 to the next one. A fixture that would sit across that line is refused the address instead of quietly breaking in the middle of a show.

How to use it

Programming

Build the look. Store it. Reuse it all night.

Every reusable thing in DMXexpress has its own pool window, and every pool works the same way: make it in the Programmer, name it, store it. Here is what that actually looks like.

The one thing that trips everybody up.

The Programmer sits on top of all playback. While a channel is in it, you control that channel — not the cues. So when the faders and the Go button seem to do nothing, it is almost always the Programmer still holding those channels. Press Clear prog, or type clear, and playback takes the stage back.

Playback

Run it with faders, with pads, or with eight keystrokes.

Record your looks as cues, then play the show however the night wants it: a bottom strip of faders, a page of pads you busk from, or a command line you can drive without looking.

Type it faster than you can click it.

One box at the bottom of the window, one verb, Enter. Numbers are 1-based and in pool order, and anything the command line does not understand is reported in the Log rather than swallowed.

Type thisAnd it does
go · go 2Go on the current stack, or on stack 2
off · off 2Release every stack, or release just that one
cue 3Jump the current stack to cue position 3
storeStore a cue into the current stack
group 1Recall group 1 as your selection
gm 80 · fullGrand Master to 80%, or back up to 100%
clearRelease the Programmer so playback takes the output back
black · boBlackout: release every stack and clear the Programmer

Out of the computer

Art-Net, sACN, and a screen that says why it isn't working.

Pick a protocol — Art-Net, sACN, or both at once — and an interface, and DMXexpress streams two contiguous universes at about 40 frames a second. When a node stays dark, the Network screen tells you what is wrong in plain words instead of leaving you to guess.

NW

Two protocols

Art-Net, sACN, or both together. Broadcast, directed broadcast to the interface's own subnet, unicast to one node, or a unicast copy to every node that answered an ArtPoll.

CK

A live checklist

One finding per row, graded by how bad it is, saying what is wrong and what to do about it. It reads the same state the rest of the screen shows, so it is never out of date.

TM

Test and monitor

Send every slot full on, walk one slot up the universe, or hold a single channel — for as long as you set, then the show comes back. The monitor keeps the last fifty packets.

Check the patch before you check the lights

DMXexpress transmits live lighting data. Confirm the interface, the universe numbers, the patch and the Grand Master before you plug into a rig with people standing under it.

Gobos

The beam is the picture, not a cone.

The visualizer renders each beam through the gobo that light is actually showing. Haze inside the cone breaks into the gobo's rays, and the pool where the beam lands is the projected image, turning with the fixture's gobo rotation channel.

CT

A thousand pictures

The catalogue is built from QLC+ and the Open Fixture Library: about a thousand images filed by maker — Chauvet, Clay Paky, GLP, Robe, SGM and a generic set — plus a named set with keywords.

WH

Wheels matched for you

When the patch is built, every gobo wheel channel is matched against those definitions by maker and model, so your lights arrive with their real slots already on them. A slot with no picture throws a plain cone.

PN

Or bring your own

Press Assign pictures and click a tile to choose what that slot projects, searching the catalogue by name, keyword and maker. Drop a PNG into fixtures/gobos/ and it turns up there too.

Get started

Build it and run it.

You'll need a current Rust toolchain with Cargo, a GPU supported by wgpu, and macOS or a compatible Linux desktop. An Art-Net node is only needed for live output — the visualizer works with nothing else on the network.

Quick start
# run in development
cargo run

# optimized build
cargo build --release
./target/release/dmxpress

# run the tests
cargo test

Network safety: DMXexpress can transmit live lighting data over Art-Net. Confirm the selected interface, universe, patch, and Grand Master before connecting to a live rig.
